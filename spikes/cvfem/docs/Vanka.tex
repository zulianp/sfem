\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=red!4,colframe=red!55!black,title=\bfseries Why this document exists]
Measured on our solver, the block-Jacobi smoother has an asymptotic convergence factor of
$\rho \approx 0.981$ at $\Rey = 1$ --- 1.9\,\% error removal per sweep, 120 sweeps per decade
--- and \emph{diverges} for $\omega \ge 0.5$. The SIMPLE variant measures identically. Local
Fourier analysis for Stokes puts a Vanka smoother at $\rho = 0.08$.

That difference, roughly a factor of 50 in iteration count, is the single largest lever
available to this solver.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{What Vanka is, and why block-Jacobi fails here}

\subsection{The saddle-point structure}
Our linearised system has the block form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A} =
\begin{pmatrix} F & B^{\mathsf T} \\ B & -C \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{pmatrix} \delta u \\ \delta p \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $F$ is the momentum operator (viscous plus convective), $B$ the discrete divergence,
$B^{\mathsf T}$ the pressure gradient, and $C$ the Rhie--Chow stabilisation. Without
stabilisation $C = 0$ and the system is a genuine saddle point.

A \emph{point-block} smoother inverts, at each node $i$, the $4\times4$ block
\begin{equation}
D_i =
\begin{pmatrix}
F_{ii} & B^{\mathsf T}_{ii} \\ B_{ii} & -C_{ii}
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
and this is where it fails. The pressure row of $D_i$ contains only the \emph{local} part of
the divergence constraint. Continuity is a statement about fluxes across the faces
\emph{surrounding} a node, so $B_{ii}$ carries almost none of it: the constraint that actually
determines the pressure lives in the off-diagonal couplings $B_{ij}$, $j \ne i$, which the
point-block smoother discards entirely. Damping the momentum equation while ignoring the
constraint is why $\rho \to 1$: the velocity error is smoothed, and the pressure error is
barely touched.

\subsection{The Vanka idea}
Vanka's construction (S.\,P.\ Vanka, \emph{JCP} \textbf{65} (1986) 138--158) replaces the
per-node block by a small \emph{coupled local system} over a patch $\Omega_k$ of degrees of
freedom chosen so that at least one pressure unknown appears \emph{together with all the
velocity unknowns that its continuity equation references}. Writing $R_k$ for the restriction
onto patch $k$, one sweep of additive Vanka is
\begin{equation}
x \leftarrow x + \omega \sum_k R_k^{\mathsf T}\,
\big( R_k \mathcal{A} R_k^{\mathsf T} \big)^{-1} R_k \, (b - \mathcal{A}x).
\label{eq:vanka}
\end{equation}
The inverted object $\mathcal{A}_k = R_k \mathcal{A} R_k^{\mathsf T}$ is the true operator
restricted to the patch, \emph{including} the velocity--pressure coupling. That is the whole
difference from block-Jacobi, and it is why the local solve can enforce the constraint.

\section{Choosing the patch for a colocated CVFEM discretisation}

Vanka was devised for staggered (MAC) grids, where the patch is obvious: one cell-centred
pressure plus the six face velocities that its continuity equation uses. Our discretisation is
\emph{colocated} --- all four fields live at every node --- so the patch must be chosen
deliberately.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrl@{}}
\toprule
Patch & Size & Comment \\
\midrule
Node star & $1 + 27\times3 = 82$ & one pressure, velocities of the $3^3$ neighbourhood \\
\textbf{Micro-element} & $8\times4 = \mathbf{32}$ & the 8 corners of one micro-cell, all fields \\
Pressure Schur only & 8 & micro-element patch with velocities eliminated \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=green!4,colframe=green!45!black,title=\bfseries Recommendation: the micro-element patch]
The micro-element patch is the right choice here for a reason specific to our code: the
$32\times32$ patch operator $\mathcal{A}_k$ is \emph{exactly the dense micro-cell matrix we
already know how to build}. Passing identity slots to
\code{cvfem\_hex8\_ns\_upwind\_jacobian\_add\_slots} writes it into a local buffer --- the same
trick \code{assemble\_block\_diag} uses and the same one the element-wise Galerkin assembly
relies on. No new kernel is needed to form the patch operator.

It also aligns with every structure the solver already has: the micro-cell loop, the constant
corner offsets on the lattice, and the deterministic two-pass scatter.
\end{tcolorbox}

A subtlety in our favour: because Rhie--Chow supplies $C \neq 0$, the patch matrix has a
non-zero pressure block and is nonsingular without any extra pinning. On an unstabilised
saddle point the local systems are singular up to a constant and need care; we avoid that.

\section{Variants, and what each costs}

\subsection{Full versus diagonal Vanka}
\emph{Full} Vanka inverts $\mathcal{A}_k$ exactly. \emph{Diagonal} Vanka approximates the
velocity block $F_k$ by its diagonal, which lets the velocities be eliminated analytically and
leaves a small pressure system --- the local Schur complement
\begin{equation}
S_k = -C_k - B_k\,\mathrm{diag}(F_k)^{-1} B_k^{\mathsf T},
\end{equation}
of size $8\times8$ for the micro-element patch. This is the variant most production codes use;
it retains the essential velocity--pressure coupling while removing most of the cost.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=orange!6,colframe=orange!60!black,title={\bfseries The trade, stated plainly}]
The local solve is a small part of a sweep --- the sweep is dominated by the residual
evaluation, which is unchanged. Diagonal Vanka should raise sweep cost by tens of percent, not
multiples. Against that, moving $\rho$ from 0.98 to even 0.3 is a factor of $\sim$50 in
iteration count. Full Vanka's 432\,MiB of cached factors is the reason to start with the
diagonal variant, not its flop count.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{High-performance implementation on the semi-structured mesh}

\subsection{Additive versus multiplicative, and colouring}
Equation~\eqref{eq:vanka} is \emph{additive}: every patch reads the same residual, so all
patches are independent and the sweep is embarrassingly parallel. It converges more slowly
than the \emph{multiplicative} form, in which each patch sees the updates of those before it
(Gauss--Seidel in patches).

The multiplicative form parallelises by colouring patches so that same-coloured patches share
no degrees of freedom. On our lattice this is exact and free: two micro-cells share a node iff
their indices differ by at most one in every axis, so the parity classes
\begin{equation}
c(i,j,k) = (i \bmod 2) + 2\,(j \bmod 2) + 4\,(k \bmod 2) \in \{0,\dots,7\}
\end{equation}
give \textbf{eight colours}, and within a colour every pair of cells differs by at least two in
some axis and is therefore independent. Eight sequential passes, each fully parallel over
$L^3/8$ cells per macro-element.

\subsection{Accumulation and determinism}
Additive Vanka writes overlapping contributions to shared nodes --- exactly the pattern the
solver already handles. Reuse \code{SSScatter}: each macro-element writes its exclusively owned
nodes directly and stages the shared ones, then a second pass sums each shared node's
contributions in a fixed order. No atomics, and the sweep is bitwise reproducible on any thread
count, which the existing determinism gates will check for free.

\subsection{Where the factorisations live}
The patch matrices depend on the linearisation state, so they are valid for one Newton step and
must be rebuilt when it changes --- the same lifetime as the element-wise Galerkin coarse
operators, and they can be built in the same pass.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Cache the $8\times8$ Schur factors} (27\,MiB at $L=8$): build once per Newton
step, apply on every sweep. Six sweeps per V-cycle and hundreds of cycles per Newton step make
this overwhelmingly the right trade.
\item Store them in the \emph{lattice} layout already used elsewhere --- indexed by
\code{(macro element, micro-cell)} --- so the sweep streams them contiguously with no
indirection, exactly as the 27-point stencil coarse operators do.
\item Do \emph{not} cache the full $32\times32$ factors; 432\,MiB is not worth it before the
diagonal variant has been measured.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Matrix-free, precisely}
``Matrix-free'' here means no \emph{global} sparse matrix, not no local matrices. Vanka needs
the patch entries, and we form them transiently per micro-cell from the existing kernel, use
them, and discard them --- which is what the element-wise Galerkin assembly already does. The
fine-level residual evaluation, which dominates the sweep, stays exactly as it is.

\section{Implementation plan}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Form the patch operator.} Per micro-cell, identity slots into
\code{cvfem\_hex8\_ns\_upwind\_jacobian\_add\_slots} give the dense $32\times32$
$\mathcal{A}_k$. Already available; no new kernel.
\item \textbf{Build the local Schur complement.} Extract $\mathrm{diag}(F_k)$, form
$S_k = -C_k - B_k\,\mathrm{diag}(F_k)^{-1}B_k^{\mathsf T}$ ($8\times8$), factor it, and store
by \code{(element, cell)}. One pass per Newton step, fusable with the Galerkin assembly pass.
\item \textbf{Additive sweep first.} Residual, then per cell: gather 32 local values, solve
$S_k$ for the pressure correction, back-substitute the velocities, scatter through
\code{SSScatter}. Additive is simpler and its correctness is easier to establish.
\item \textbf{Measure with \code{SFEM\_GMG\_CHECK=3}}, 150 sweeps, to the asymptote --- the same
gate that produced the 0.981 figure, so the comparison is like for like. Sweep $\omega$.
\item \textbf{Then multiplicative with 8-colour parity} if the additive rate is insufficient.
Expect a substantially better factor at the cost of eight sequential passes.
\item \textbf{Only then reconsider full Vanka}, and only if the diagonal variant's rate is
close enough to make the memory worth it.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!4,colframe=blue!50!black,title=\bfseries Acceptance criterion]
The measurement that matters is the standalone smoother rate at the \emph{physical} Reynolds
number, run to its asymptote with no coarse space. Block-Jacobi gives 0.981 at $\Rey=1$. Any
Vanka variant that does not comfortably beat that, at $\Rey \ge 100$, is not worth carrying ---
and a rate near or above 1 must be treated as disqualifying however good it looks over the
first few sweeps, because the rate can fall for twenty sweeps and then climb through unity.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{The smoother as implemented: set-up and apply}
\label{sec:implemented}

The plan above is implemented in \code{src/ss/cvfem\_ss\_vanka.hpp}: diagonal Vanka on the
micro-element patch, multiplicative inside a macro-element and additive between
macro-elements, used on every smoothed multigrid level. It differs from the plan in two ways.
First, the patch operator is read from the \emph{assembled} operator, not from one
micro-cell's contribution. Second, the coarse levels run the same code on the Galerkin
operators the hierarchy already builds. This section gives the set-up and the apply at the
level of detail needed to reason about their cost or to reimplement them.

\subsection{Notation and data}

\paragraph{Lattice.} There are $n_M$ macro-elements at level $L$. Each has $(L+1)^3$ lattice
nodes and $L^3$ micro-cells. Lattice node $(x,y,z)$ has local index
$\ell(x,y,z) = x + (L+1)\,\big(y + (L+1)\,z\big)$, and $g_e(\ell)$ is its global node in
macro-element $e$. The eight corners of micro-cell $(x,y,z)$ are $\ell(x,y,z) + o_a$ with
\begin{equation}
o = \big(0,\; 1,\; L{+}2,\; L{+}1,\; (L{+}1)^2,\; (L{+}1)^2{+}1,\; (L{+}1)^2{+}L{+}2,\;
(L{+}1)^2{+}L{+}1\big).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Operator.} $A$ is stored in BSR with $4\times4$ blocks, sorted column indices, and
at most 27 blocks per row. Fields are ordered $(u_x,u_y,u_z,p)$ and each block is row-major.
For nodes $i,j$ the block splits as
\begin{equation}
A_{ij} = \begin{pmatrix} F_{ij} & G_{ij} \\ D_{ij} & A^{pp}_{ij} \end{pmatrix},
\qquad F_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times3},\; G_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\; D_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times3},
\; A^{pp}_{ij} = -C_{ij}.
\end{equation}
$G$ is the pressure gradient ($B^{\mathsf T}$ above) and $D$ the divergence ($B$). The patch
operator of cell $k$, with corner nodes $n_1,\dots,n_8$, is the $32\times32$ matrix of blocks
$A_{n_a n_b}$. On the fine level, $A$ comes from the element-wise Galerkin assembly at
ratio $q=1$. There the prolongation is the identity and $P^{\mathsf T}AP = A$, and that path
matches the matrix-free apply to $1.54\times10^{-16}$. On a coarse level, $A$ is that level's
Galerkin coarse operator. Each level passes its own mask of constrained degrees of freedom.

\paragraph{Why the assembled operator.} A node inside a macro-element lies in eight
micro-cells, so a single cell's element matrix carries about an eighth of the true patch
entries. A smoother built on it diverged at a rate of 18. Taking only the diagonal from the
assembled matrix and the off-diagonal entries from the cell made the patch system internally
inconsistent, and that version diverged at 7.8.

\paragraph{Per-cell record.} Set-up stores the following per micro-cell, in lattice order
$(e, z, y, x)$, so the sweep streams the records contiguously. $T$ is the storage type
(\S\ref{sec:precision}).
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Field & Shape & Contents & Used for \\
\midrule
$\hat D$ & $8\times24$ & $D_{n_a n_i}[t] / F_{n_i n_i}[t,t]$ & pressure right-hand side \\
$\hat G$ & $24\times8$ & $G_{n_i n_b}[t] / F_{n_i n_i}[t,t]$ & velocity back-substitution \\
$d^{-1}$ & $24$ & $1 / F_{n_i n_i}[t,t]$ (floored) & velocity back-substitution \\
$S$, $\pi$ & $8\times8$, $8$ & LU factors and pivots of the Schur complement & pressure solve \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
That is 472 values of type $T$ plus 8 integers: 1\,920\,B in single precision and
3\,808\,B in double. At $L=8$ with 420 macro-elements there are 215\,040 cells, which is
394\,MiB in single precision and 781\,MiB in double.

\subsection{Set-up}

Set-up runs once per Newton step, because the patch operators depend on the linearisation.
This is the same lifetime as the Galerkin coarse operators.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Vanka set-up, \code{vanka\_setup}}\label{alg:setup}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require assembled BSR operator $A$; constraint mask; tolerances $\tau_d = \tau_S = 10^{-14}$
\State $w_i \gets 1/\#\{\text{micro-cells containing node } i\}$
  \Comment{additive form}
\State $w^{E}_i \gets 1/\#\{\text{macro-elements containing node } i\}$
  \Comment{multiplicative form}
\ForAll{macro-elements $e$ \textbf{in parallel}}
  \ForAll{micro-cells $k=(x,y,z)$ of $e$}
    \State $n_a \gets g_e(\ell(x,y,z) + o_a)$, \quad $a = 1,\dots,8$
    \For{$a, b = 1,\dots,8$} \Comment{64 blocks, double precision}
      \State $\mathcal{A}[a][b] \gets A_{n_a n_b}$, found by binary search in row $n_a$
        (zero if absent)
    \EndFor
    \State $s \gets \max_{a,t} |F_{n_a n_a}[t,t]|$ \Comment{the patch's own velocity scale}
    \For{$a = 1,\dots,8$, $t = 1,2,3$}
      \State $\delta \gets F_{n_a n_a}[t,t]$
      \State $d^{-1}_{a,t} \gets 1/\delta$ \textbf{if} $|\delta| > \tau_d\, s$ \textbf{else} $0$
    \EndFor
    \State $\hat D_{a,(i,t)} \gets D_{n_a n_i}[t]\; d^{-1}_{i,t}$, \quad
           $\hat G_{(i,t),b} \gets G_{n_i n_b}[t]\; d^{-1}_{i,t}$
    \State $S_{ab} \gets A^{pp}_{n_a n_b}
           - \sum_{i=1}^{8}\sum_{t=1}^{3} D_{n_a n_i}[t]\; d^{-1}_{i,t}\; G_{n_i n_b}[t]$
    \State factor $S = P^{\mathsf T} L U$ with partial pivoting; reject pivot
           $\le \tau_S \max_{ab}|S_{ab}|$
    \If{factorisation rejected}
      \State $S \gets I$, \; $\pi \gets \mathrm{id}$ \Comment{withhold this patch's pressure update}
    \EndIf
    \State narrow $\hat D, \hat G, d^{-1}, S$ to $T$ and store in the record of $k$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\If{$T$ is narrower than the assembled values}
  \State allocate $\tilde A$ uninitialised and fill $\tilde A \gets (T)\,A$ \textbf{in parallel}
    \Comment{first touch by the sweeping threads}
\Else
  \State $\tilde A \gets A$ \Comment{alias, no copy}
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{The safeguards.} Each one records a failure that was observed:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{The velocity-diagonal floor is relative.} An absolute floor of $10^{-300}$ let a
diagonal of $3.6\times10^{-17}$ through at a do-nothing outflow node, where the mass flux
across the face is near zero. The resulting $d^{-1} = 2.8\times10^{16}$ was exactly the
per-sweep amplification of the unstable smoother, and it was independent of $\omega$. The
floor is far below any legitimate value: at $10^{-11}$ it zeroed healthy diagonals and took
Poiseuille flow from 178 to 1\,119 linear iterations. The optional
\code{SFEM\_VANKA\_ROW\_FRAC} bounds $|\delta|$ below by a fraction of the row's $\ell^1$ norm
over the patch velocities. It is off by default and bit-identical when off.
\item \emph{The pivot tolerance is relative.} It is measured against the Schur complement's
own largest entry, for the same reason. A pivot of $10^{-20}$ in a complement of scale
$10^{-3}$ once returned values of about $10^{17}$ from the preconditioner.
\item \emph{A rejected patch falls back to the identity.} This makes the smoother weaker for
that patch rather than explosive.
\item \emph{Arithmetic is in double.} Every product and the factorisation are computed in
double precision, and results are narrowed to $T$ only when stored.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Cost.} Each cell does 64 binary searches over at most 27 columns, forms the Schur
complement with $8\cdot8\cdot24 = 1\,536$ triple products, and factors an $8\times8$
($\approx 170$ multiply-adds). The loop has no shared writes and is parallel over
macro-elements. On the FDA nozzle at 893\,924 dof ($L=8$, 215\,040 cells, 72 Grace cores),
the fine-level set-up takes 5.1\,s over 28 Newton steps, which is 0.18\,s per step and 8\,\%
of the run.

\subsection{The patch solve}

The additive and multiplicative applies share one local solve. Given the patch residual
$r_u \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$ and $r_p \in \mathbb{R}^{8}$, eliminating the velocities through
$\mathrm{diag}(F_k)$ gives
\begin{equation}
S\,\delta p = r_p - \hat D\, r_u, \qquad
\delta u = d^{-1} \odot r_u - \hat G\, \delta p .
\label{eq:patchsolve}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Patch solve}\label{alg:patch}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require cell record $(\hat D, \hat G, d^{-1}, S, \pi)$; $r_u$, $r_p$ in double
\State $\delta p_a \gets r_{p,a} - \sum_{j=1}^{24} \hat D_{a j}\, r_{u,j}$
  \Comment{192 multiply-adds}
\State apply $\pi$ to $\delta p$; forward-substitute with $L$; back-substitute with $U$
  \Comment{64}
\State $\delta u_j \gets d^{-1}_j\, r_{u,j} - \sum_{b=1}^{8} \hat G_{j b}\, \delta p_b$
  \Comment{216}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
The stored values are promoted to double as they are read, so the local arithmetic is
double whatever $T$ is.

\subsection{Additive apply}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Additive Vanka, \code{vanka\_apply}: $y \gets y + \omega\, W \sum_k R_k^{\mathsf T}
\hat{\mathcal A}_k^{-1} R_k\, r$}\label{alg:add}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $z \gets 0$
\ForAll{macro-elements $e$ \textbf{in parallel}}
  \State gather $r_\ell \gets r_{g_e(\ell)}$ for all $(L+1)^3$ lattice nodes; $z^{e} \gets 0$
  \ForAll{micro-cells $k$ of $e$}
    \State restrict $r^e$ to the 8 corners $\to (r_u, r_p)$
    \State $(\delta u, \delta p) \gets$ Algorithm~\ref{alg:patch}
    \State $z^e_{\ell + o_a} \mathrel{+}= (\delta u_a, \delta p_a)$ for $a = 1,\dots,8$
  \EndFor
  \State two-pass scatter, pass 1: write $z^e$ to nodes owned by $e$ alone; stage shared nodes
\EndFor
\State two-pass scatter, pass 2: sum staged contributions per shared node in a fixed order
\ForAll{nodes $i$, fields $t$, not constrained}
  \State $y_{i,t} \mathrel{+}= \omega\, w_i\, z_{i,t}$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
Every patch reads the same residual, so the cells are independent. The weight $w_i$ averages
over the patches containing a node. Without it, a node inside a macro-element would receive
eight times the correction of a lattice corner.

\subsection{Multiplicative apply}

This is the default (\code{SFEM\_VANKA\_MULT=1}). Standalone at $\Rey = 1$ it measures
$\rho = 0.63$ at $\omega = 1$, against 0.883 for the additive form.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Multiplicative Vanka, \code{vanka\_apply\_mult}}\label{alg:mult}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $z \gets 0$
\ForAll{macro-elements $e$ \textbf{in parallel}} \Comment{thread-private $\lambda$, initially $-1$}
  \For{$\ell = 1,\dots,(L+1)^3$}
    \State $\lambda(g_e(\ell)) \gets \ell$; \; $r^e_\ell \gets r_{g_e(\ell)}$
  \EndFor
  \State $z^e \gets 0$
  \For{colour $c = 0,\dots,7$} \Comment{$c = c_x + 2c_y + 4c_z$}
    \ForAll{micro-cells $k = (x,y,z)$ with $(x,y,z) \equiv (c_x,c_y,c_z) \pmod 2$}
      \For{$a = 1,\dots,8$} \Comment{local residual from the current local iterate}
        \State $\ell_a \gets \ell(x,y,z) + o_a$, \; $\rho \gets r^e_{\ell_a}$
        \ForAll{blocks $(j, \tilde A_{g_e(\ell_a) j})$ in BSR row $g_e(\ell_a)$}
          \If{$\lambda(j) \ge 0$}
            \State $\rho \gets \rho - \tilde A_{g_e(\ell_a) j}\; z^e_{\lambda(j)}$
          \EndIf \Comment{columns outside $e$ are skipped}
        \EndFor
        \State $(r_{u,a}, r_{p,a}) \gets \rho$
      \EndFor
      \State $(\delta u, \delta p) \gets$ Algorithm~\ref{alg:patch}
      \State $z^e_{\ell_a} \mathrel{+}= \omega\,(\delta u_a, \delta p_a)$ for $a = 1,\dots,8$
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
  \State two-pass scatter, pass 1 of $z^e$
  \For{$\ell = 1,\dots,(L+1)^3$}
    \State $\lambda(g_e(\ell)) \gets -1$ \Comment{reset costs $(L+1)^3$, not $n_\text{nodes}$}
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State two-pass scatter, pass 2
\ForAll{nodes $i$, fields $t$, not constrained}
  \State $y_{i,t} \mathrel{+}= w^{E}_i\, z_{i,t}$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Why multiplicative only inside a macro-element.} The eight-colour argument holds
inside one macro-element: two micro-cells there share a node exactly when their indices
differ by at most one on every axis. It fails across macro-elements, where same-coloured
cells share face nodes. A sweep that ran colours globally in parallel over macro-elements
raced on those nodes, and the determinism gate caught it: 36 linear iterations on one thread
against 156 on eight. Each macro-element therefore sweeps its own cells in Gauss--Seidel
order on a private vector. Its update reads only its own nodes, because the map $\lambda$
drops every column outside $e$. The corrections are then combined additively through the
two-pass scatter and averaged with $w^{E}$. There are no atomics, and the result is
bit-identical on any thread count.

\paragraph{Cost, and why it is memory bound.} The patch solve is about 470 multiply-adds per
cell. The local residual walks 8 BSR rows of up to 27 blocks, which is up to
$8\cdot27\cdot16 = 3\,456$ multiply-adds and 216 indirect lookups per cell, reading 64 bytes
per block in single precision. The row walk is therefore the larger cost, and it reads memory
in a pattern the prefetcher does not follow. On the nozzle at 893\,924 dof the fine-level
sweep was 72\,\% of the socket's cycles, and 72\,\% of those were stalled in the back end.
The bytes streamed, not the flop count, set the speed of the sweep.

\subsection{How the multigrid cycle calls it}

Each smoothed level wraps the smoother $P$ (Algorithm~\ref{alg:mult}, or \ref{alg:add}) in a
stationary iteration with $\nu = 2$ sweeps (\code{SFEM\_GMG\_SMOOTH}), before and after the
coarse correction. The coarsest level is solved by dense LU. Because $P$ accumulates into its
output,
\begin{equation}
x^{(m+1)} = x^{(m)} + P\big(b - A\,x^{(m)}\big), \qquad m = 0,\dots,\nu-1 .
\end{equation}
The first pre-smoothing sweep of a cycle always starts from $x^{(0)} = 0$: FGMRES passes a
zero vector to the preconditioner, and coarse levels start from zero by construction. In that
case the iteration is told so (\code{set\_initial\_guess\_zero}) and takes $x^{(1)} = P\,b$
directly. This saves one operator application per level per cycle and gives the same bits.

The defaults are $\omega = 1$ (\code{SFEM\_VANKA\_OMEGA}) with the multiplicative form. An open
do-nothing outlet changes this: inside the cycle it sets $\omega = 0.5$, and without multigrid
it selects the additive form. An explicitly set variable always takes precedence. On the
fine level, the set-up assembles $A$ through the Galerkin path at $q = 1$
(\code{make\_diagonal\_vanka}). On a coarse level it reads the Galerkin BSR that level already
holds (\code{make\_diagonal\_vanka\_from\_bsr}).

\subsection{Storage precision}
\label{sec:precision}

The storage type $T$ of the cell records and of the row walk's values $\tilde A$ is set by
the environment variable \code{SFEM\_VANKA\_PRECISION}. It is either \code{single}
(the default, $T$ = \code{float}) or \code{double}. Both compute in double, and both are the same templated code. Single is the default because it
measures faster. Measured on the warped FDA nozzle at throat $\Rey = 500$, FGMRES with the
geometric multigrid, 72 Grace cores. Every run had the same Newton and linear iterations, flux
and station velocities:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rlrr@{}}
\toprule
dof & storage & fine-level smoothing & run \\
\midrule
116\,212 & single & 1.98\,s & 7.23\,s \\
116\,212 & double & 2.40\,s & 7.82\,s \\
893\,924 & single & 40.2\,s & 65.8\,s \\
893\,924 & double & 70.1\,s & 96.6\,s \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At 893\,924 dof one fine-level smoothing step costs 17.4\,ms in single precision and 30.4\,ms
in double. A step is two sweeps plus the operator application between them, and that operator
application does not change with $T$. The factor of 1.75 over the whole step is therefore a
lower bound for the sweeps alone. It is close to the factor of two in bytes streamed, which is
further evidence that the sweep is memory bound. A smoother only has to reduce the error, and FGMRES
absorbs the difference.

\section{Assembling the patches without the global BSR}
\label{sec:nobsr}

\subsection{What the BSR is used for today}

The fine-level smoother builds a global operator only to read it back in pieces. The pipeline is:
\begin{sloppypar}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt]
\item element matrices of every micro-cell, at ratio $q=1$ of the element-wise Galerkin assembly,
  accumulated into 27 fixed stencil slots per lattice node of each macro-element;
\item those slots accumulated into a global BSR through the cached pattern, in
  \code{assemble\_coarse\_operator};
\item set-up: 64 binary searches per patch to find its blocks in the BSR (Algorithm~\ref{alg:setup});
\item the multiplicative sweep: a walk over each patch corner's BSR row, through the column index
  array, through the map $\lambda$ that drops every column outside the current macro-element
  (Algorithm~\ref{alg:mult}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{sloppypar}
\begin{sloppypar}
The double-precision BSR is kept alive by the smoother, and at single precision a float copy of its
values is kept beside it for the row walk. Two observations make the BSR avoidable. The sweep
never reads a column outside the macro-element it is sweeping. And the Galerkin element-matrix path
already holds and applies an operator as per-macro-element 27-slot stencils, summing shared nodes
with the deterministic two-pass reduction (\code{galerkin\_apply}, \code{galerkin\_block\_diag},
\code{GalerkinReduce}).
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Is Vanka a Navier--Stokes smoother, or only a Stokes one?}

It is a Navier--Stokes smoother by origin: Vanka's 1986 papers solve the primitive-variable
Navier--Stokes equations on recirculating cavity flows, not Stokes, and Vanka-type smoothers
are standard in finite-element Navier--Stokes multigrid. The ``Stokes smoother'' impression
comes from the \emph{analysis}, not the method --- local Fourier analysis needs a linear,
constant-coefficient operator, so published LFA rates are almost always Stokes rates.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=orange!6,colframe=orange!60!black,title=\bfseries What the 0.08 does and does not mean]
The LFA rates quoted in this document measure how well the smoother handles the
\textbf{saddle-point coupling}. They say nothing about \textbf{convection dominance}, which is
a second and independent difficulty:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt]
\item At low $\Rey$ the momentum block $F$ is essentially elliptic, and a local patch solve is
a good local approximation --- the regime the LFA describes.
\item As $\Rey$ rises, $F$ becomes convection-dominated and nearly hyperbolic. Information
travels along streamlines, which is exactly what a \emph{local} smoother cannot follow. The
classical remedies are flow-aligned (downstream) ordering of the multiplicative sweep, or line
and plane smoothers, or semi-coarsening --- not a larger patch.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Why it is still the right first step here}
Our measured failure is at $\Rey = 1$: $\rho = 0.981$ in what is essentially the Stokes limit.
That is a pure saddle-point-coupling failure, precisely the thing Vanka fixes, and it has to be
fixed before Reynolds robustness is a meaningful question at all. Two separate problems are
being conflated if we skip it:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Symptom & Regime & Remedy \\
\midrule
$\rho = 0.981$ at $\Rey=1$ & Stokes limit & \textbf{Vanka} (this document) \\
divergence at $\Rey \ge 400$ & convection-dominated & AL preconditioner, or ordering/semi-coarsening \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{What Vanka is unlikely to do}
It should not be assumed that Vanka alone reaches $\Rey \ge 400$. For Reynolds robustness the
reference result is the augmented-Lagrangian preconditioner of Farrell, Mitchell \& Wechsung
(\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.03315}{arXiv:1810.03315}), which holds 4--8 Krylov iterations
per Newton step to $\Rey = 10\,000$. That is not a drop-in replacement for a smoother: the
augmented term $\gamma B^{\mathsf T}W^{-1}B$ makes the momentum block nearly singular, with a
large kernel, and their multigrid uses a \emph{kernel-capturing subspace-correction} smoother
built for that structure rather than Vanka.

The realistic plan is therefore two-stage: Vanka to recover a working smoother in the
Stokes-limit sense, then reassess Reynolds robustness on the evidence, with augmented
Lagrangian as the expected second step if the Re sweep still degrades.

\section{Risks and open questions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Colocated patches are not the classical construction.} Vanka's rates are
published for staggered discretisations. The micro-element patch is the natural colocated
analogue, but the LFA numbers do not transfer automatically and our own measurement is the
only evidence that will count.
\item \textbf{Rhie--Chow coupling.} $C \neq 0$ makes the patches nonsingular, which helps, but
the stabilisation scale $D_f = \text{rc}\,h^2/(2\mu)$ grows as $\Rey$ rises and as the mesh
coarsens; the patch conditioning should be checked across the hierarchy, not only on the fine
level.
\item \textbf{Convection dominance} --- see the section above. No Stokes rate speaks to it,
and measuring the Reynolds dependence of whatever smoother we adopt is not optional.
\item \textbf{Coarse levels.} The coarse levels are assembled BSR, not matrix-free, so their
patch operators must be extracted from the assembled matrix instead of from the cell kernel ---
a second, simpler code path.
\end{itemize}
