\section{The convective schemes}
\label{sec:schemes}

The schemes differ only in $\mathbf{h}_\scs$, the higher-order correction in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:flux}. All five share the mass flux, the upwind split and the pressure term.

\subsection{The reconstruction and the increment}
\label{sec:schemes:recon}

A sub-control surface $\scs$ separates two nodes $i$ and $j$ and has a centroid $\xvec^c_\scs$.
For each velocity component $u^{(k)}$, $k \in \{x,y,z\}$, the face value is reconstructed from a
node and that node's \emph{nodal} velocity gradient:
\begin{equation}
u^{(k)}_{\text{face},a} \;=\; u^{(k)}_a \;+\; \Delta^{(k)}_a ,
\qquad
\Delta^{(k)}_a \;=\; \bigl(\Gop u^{(k)}\bigr)_a \cdot \bigl(\xvec^c_\scs - \xvec_a\bigr),
\qquad a \in \{i,j\}.
\label{eq:increment}
\end{equation}
Here $\Gop u$ is the nodal gradient reconstruction of Section~\ref{sec:recon}: nine components
per node, one gradient per velocity component. \emph{This is the one place in the residual where a
velocity gradient is needed}, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:increment} is why --- without a nodal gradient
there is nothing to extrapolate along $\xvec^c_\scs - \xvec_a$, and the face value collapses to
the nodal one, which is first-order upwinding.

Each increment is passed through a limiter,
\begin{equation}
\delta^{(k)}_a \;=\; \Lambda\!\left(\Delta^{(k)}_a;\; u^{(k)}_i,\, u^{(k)}_j\right),
\label{eq:limited}
\end{equation}
whose bounds come from the surface's own two nodes, $[\,\ell, h\,] =
[\min(u_i,u_j),\,\max(u_i,u_j)]$ --- a two-node stencil, not a full one-ring. With $\Lambda$ the
identity this is unlimited second-order upwinding; the three limiters below are the three
non-trivial choices.

\subsection{Deferred correction}

The correction is the \emph{difference} between the reconstructed flux and the first-order upwind
one. Since first-order upwinding takes $u_a$ at the donor and the reconstruction takes
$u_a + \delta_a$, that difference is $\delta_a$ carried by the same upwind split:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_\scs \;=\; \mdot^{+}\,\boldsymbol{\delta}_i \;+\; \mdot^{-}\,\boldsymbol{\delta}_j .
\label{eq:defcor}
\end{equation}
Adding this to Eq.~\eqref{eq:flux} gives the higher-order flux exactly. Note which mass flux
weights it: $\mdot$ is the one Eq.~\eqref{eq:mdot} returned, Rhie--Chow term included, so the
correction is transported by the flux that is actually there rather than by a second opinion
about it.

What makes the scheme \emph{deferred} is how Eq.~\eqref{eq:defcor} is treated in the implicit
operator. The first-order flux is carried implicitly and $\mathbf{h}_\scs$ explicitly, as a
source~\cite{KhoslaRubin1974,XuePhanThienTanner2002}, so the implicit operator stays the
first-order upwind one: diagonally dominant, one-ring, and \emph{the same for every scheme in
this section}. The higher-order accuracy is recovered through the outer iteration. That is the
property the method exists for, and Section~\ref{sec:jac:defcor} is where it is paid for.

\subsection{Scheme 0: unlimited}

$\Lambda(\Delta) = \Delta$. The arm every other is measured against, and the solver's default.
Smooth, differentiable, and does not preserve the bound: a reconstruction can place the face value
outside the interval its nodes span.

\subsection{Scheme 1: bounded-face clip}

Barth--Jespersen's bound on the two-node stencil
(\code{cvfem\_limiter\_clip\_inc}):
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(\Delta) \;=\; \operatorname{clip}\bigl(u_a + \Delta,\; \ell,\; h\bigr) - u_a .
\end{equation}
The face value cannot introduce a new extremum, so the bound holds by construction. It is kept as
the reference arm rather than recommended: it clips at a smooth extremum too, and its active set
flips --- an ulp of change in $\Delta$ can move the result by a finite amount, which is exactly
what makes its derivative delicate (Section~\ref{sec:jac:subgrad}).

\subsection{Scheme 2: Venkatakrishnan}

A smooth limiter (\code{cvfem\_venkata\_inc}). With $D^{+}$ the signed room to the bound in the
direction of the increment,
\begin{equation}
D^{+} = \begin{cases} h - u_a, & \Delta \ge 0\\ \ell - u_a, & \Delta < 0\end{cases}
\qquad
\Lambda(\Delta) \;=\; \frac{(D^{+})^2 + 2\Delta D^{+} + \varepsilon^2}
                          {(D^{+})^2 + 2\Delta^2 + \Delta D^{+} + \varepsilon^2}\;\Delta .
\label{eq:venkat}
\end{equation}
The denominator is a sum of squares, $(D^{+} + \Delta/2)^2 + \tfrac74\Delta^2 + \varepsilon^2$,
hence positive unless $D^{+}$, $\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$ all vanish --- and there $\Lambda = 0$
anyway, so the guard can be placed on $\Delta$ rather than on the denominator. That is not a
cosmetic choice: writing it as a test on the denominator builds a relational around a
\code{Piecewise}, which the code generator folds into a ternary \emph{inside the condition of
another ternary}, and GCC then abandons the loop. The generated Venkatakrishnan kernel contained
zero vector instructions for exactly this reason until the guard was moved.

$\varepsilon^2$ is Venkatakrishnan's deactivation term, dimensional in the square of the solution
variable. Its purpose is to switch the limiter \emph{off} where the two-node variation is at the
level of round-off --- along flow-aligned edges in a channel core, where $u_i$ and $u_j$ agree to
round-off while the true face value legitimately differs from both transversally. Without it the
bound is decided by round-off there, the limiter clips a correction that was right, and refining
makes the error worse at $h^{-2}$. $\varepsilon^2 = 0$ recovers the preceding arm bit for bit,
which makes $K=0$ a genuine zero-severity control rather than an approximation of one.

\subsection{Scheme 3: Darwish--Moukalled}

van Leer's $\psi(r) = (r + |r|)/(1 + |r|)$ in the TVD form, with the limited increment
$\tfrac12\psi(r)(\phi_D - \phi_C)$. Substituting $r$ and clearing fractions
(\code{cvfem\_darwish\_moukalled\_inc}) gives
\begin{equation}
\Lambda \;=\; \frac{a\,|b| + |a|\,b}{2\,(|a| + |b|)},
\qquad a = 2\,\nabla\phi_C\cdot\dvec, \quad b = \phi_D - \phi_C ,
\label{eq:dm}
\end{equation}
which is one division rather than three and has no removable singularity: the denominator vanishes
only when $a$ and $b$ both do, and the numerator vanishes with it. Opposite signs give exactly
zero, which is $\psi(r<0) = 0$ --- the increment is discarded where the reconstruction and the
downwind difference disagree about the direction of the gradient. Note that this guard divides by
$2(|a|+|b|)$, an absolute value and not a \code{Piecewise}, which is why this kernel vectorised
when Venkatakrishnan's did not.

\subsection{The correction in the Jacobian, and the two halves of its derivative}
\label{sec:jac:defcor}

Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:defcor} along a direction $v$ is the product rule, and both factors
depend on $v$. With $\dot{\mdot} = (\partial\mdot/\partial\uvec)\,v$ and
$\partial\mdot^{\pm}/\partial\uvec\,v = \tfrac12\bigl(1 \pm \operatorname{sgn}\mdot\bigr)\dot{\mdot}$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathbf{h}_\scs}{\partial\uvec}\,v
\;=\;
\underbrace{\tfrac12(1+\operatorname{sgn}\mdot)\,\dot{\mdot}\;\boldsymbol{\delta}_i
          + \tfrac12(1-\operatorname{sgn}\mdot)\,\dot{\mdot}\;\boldsymbol{\delta}_j}_{\textbf{(a)}
          \text{ flux sensitivity} \,\times\, \text{frozen increment}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\mdot^{+}\,\dot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_i + \mdot^{-}\,\dot{\boldsymbol{\delta}}_j}_{\textbf{(b)}
          \text{ increment sensitivity}} .
\label{eq:defcor-jv}
\end{equation}
The two halves need different things, and the distinction decides which reconstructions an
operator must run.

\paragraph{(a) needs the state's velocity gradient and nothing of the direction's.}
$\boldsymbol{\delta}_a$ is Eq.~\eqref{eq:limited} evaluated at the state, so it needs
$\Gop u(u)$ --- the same values the residual's correction reads. The factor multiplying it is
$\dot{\mdot}$, the mass flux's own sensitivity, which the first-order action already computes.
This term is therefore available at no reconstruction cost beyond what $\Fho$ already pays.

\paragraph{(b) needs the direction's.} By the chain rule,
\begin{equation}
\dot{\delta}^{(k)}_a
\;=\;
\frac{\partial\Lambda}{\partial\Delta}\;
      \bigl(\Gop v^{(k)}\bigr)_a \cdot \bigl(\xvec^c_\scs - \xvec_a\bigr)
\;+\;
\frac{\partial\Lambda}{\partial u_i}\,v^{(k)}_i
\;+\;
\frac{\partial\Lambda}{\partial u_j}\,v^{(k)}_j .
\label{eq:dinc}
\end{equation}
The first piece is $\Gop v$, the direction's nodal velocity gradient, which must be rebuilt on
every operator application because the direction changes every Krylov iteration. The last two
exist only because the limiter's bounds are \emph{nodal} values: they carry the direction's nodal
values, not its gradient, and they vanish for $\Lambda$ the identity. They are the part of this
derivative a reader is most likely to drop, and the source says so where they are formed.

\paragraph{The subgradient is shared.} $\operatorname{sgn}\mdot$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:defcor-jv} is
$\partial|\mdot|/\partial\mdot$, which is the same subgradient the first-order action already
takes at $\mdot = 0$. The two linearisations therefore agree there, rather than one being exact
and the other not.

\paragraph{The Rhie--Chow coupling arrives through (a).} $\dot{\mdot}$ carries the Rhie--Chow
sensitivity of Section~\ref{sec:rc}, so when both terms are differentiated the correction's
Jacobian inherits the pressure coupling through the flux it is weighted by. The two higher-order
terms are not independent of the two pressure ones.

\subsection{Which operator needs which reconstruction}
\label{sec:schemes:needs}

Collecting Eqs.~\eqref{eq:increment}, \eqref{eq:defcor-jv} and \eqref{eq:dinc}, together with the
Rhie--Chow correction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:corr} and its derivative:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
& \multicolumn{2}{c}{pressure} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{velocity} \\
\cmidrule(lr){2-3}\cmidrule(l){4-5}
operator & $\Gop p(u)$ & $\Gop p(v)$ & $\Gop u(u)$ & $\Gop u(v)$ \\
\midrule
$\Fres(u)$, first-order residual          & \checkmark &            &            &            \\
$\Fho(u)$, higher-order residual          & \checkmark &            & \checkmark &            \\
\addlinespace
$\Jlag(u)\,v$, deferred correction        & \checkmark &            &            &            \\
$\Jex(u)\,v$, exact, first-order          & \checkmark & \checkmark &            &            \\
$\Jhoex(u)\,v$, exact, higher-order       & \checkmark & \checkmark & \checkmark & \checkmark \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
Three readings of that table are worth stating, because each is easy to get wrong.

\emph{The velocity gradient is needed in exactly two operators.} $\Fho$ needs the state's, by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:increment}. $\Jhoex$ needs the state's for term (a) and the direction's for term
(b). No other operator needs one at all --- and in particular $\Jlag$ needs none, which is the
whole of why one lagged operator serves every scheme.

\emph{A state gradient appears in every row, but it is the pressure one.} The Rhie--Chow
correction is a function of $\Gop p(u)$ whether or not its sensitivity is differentiated, so every
operator carrying the coupling reads it. That is not the same thing as needing a velocity
gradient, and conflating the two is what makes this table look inconsistent at a glance.

\emph{The two lagged forms are lagged in different senses}, which Section~\ref{sec:jac:asym}
sets out: the Rhie--Chow term freezes a gradient and is still differentiated, the correction's
derivative is dropped outright.

\subsection{Pseudocode}
\label{sec:schemes:pseudo}

The residual's correction, once per sub-control surface
(\code{cvfem\_hex8\_scs\_defcor}):

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{$\mathbf{h}_\scs$, the deferred correction (residual)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require nodes $i,j$; centroid $\xvec^c_\scs$; $\mdot$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:mdot};
         nodal state $u$; nodal gradients $\Gop u(u)$
\State $\mdot^{\pm} \gets \tfrac12(\mdot \pm |\mdot|_\epsilon)$
       \Comment{the split the flux already used}
\State $\dvec_i \gets \xvec^c_\scs - \xvec_i$; \quad $\dvec_j \gets \xvec^c_\scs - \xvec_j$
\For{each velocity component $k \in \{x,y,z\}$}
  \State $\Delta_i \gets (\Gop u^{(k)})_i \cdot \dvec_i$; \quad
         $\Delta_j \gets (\Gop u^{(k)})_j \cdot \dvec_j$
         \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:increment}}
  \State $\ell \gets \min(u^{(k)}_i, u^{(k)}_j)$; \quad $h \gets \max(u^{(k)}_i, u^{(k)}_j)$
  \State $\delta_i \gets \Lambda(\Delta_i; u^{(k)}_i, \ell, h)$; \quad
         $\delta_j \gets \Lambda(\Delta_j; u^{(k)}_j, \ell, h)$
  \State $h^{(k)}_\scs \gets \mdot^{+}\delta_i + \mdot^{-}\delta_j$
         \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:defcor}}
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent
Its derivative, once per sub-control surface
(\code{cvfem\_hex8\_scs\_defcor\_jv}). The two halves of Eq.~\eqref{eq:defcor-jv} are lines 7
and 8:

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{$(\partial\mathbf{h}_\scs/\partial\uvec)\,v$, the exact correction derivative}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require the above, plus the direction $v$, its nodal gradients $\Gop v$, and
         $\dot{\mdot}$ from the first-order action --- Rhie--Chow sensitivity included
\State $\mdot^{\pm} \gets \tfrac12(\mdot \pm |\mdot|_\epsilon)$;\quad
       $d^{\pm} \gets \tfrac12(1 \pm \operatorname{sgn}\mdot)$
\State $\dvec_i \gets \xvec^c_\scs - \xvec_i$; \quad $\dvec_j \gets \xvec^c_\scs - \xvec_j$
\For{each velocity component $k \in \{x,y,z\}$}
  \State $\Delta_a \gets (\Gop u^{(k)})_a \cdot \dvec_a$ \quad for $a \in \{i,j\}$
         \Comment{at the state}
  \State $\dot{\Delta}_a \gets (\Gop v^{(k)})_a \cdot \dvec_a$ \quad for $a \in \{i,j\}$
         \Comment{at the direction}
  \State $\delta_a,\ \dot{\delta}_a \gets \Lambda$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:dinc}, from
         $\Delta_a, \dot{\Delta}_a$ and the nodal $u^{(k)}, v^{(k)}$ at $i$ and $j$
  \State $\textbf{(a)} \gets d^{+}\dot{\mdot}\,\delta_i + d^{-}\dot{\mdot}\,\delta_j$
         \Comment{no direction gradient}
  \State $\textbf{(b)} \gets \mdot^{+}\dot{\delta}_i + \mdot^{-}\dot{\delta}_j$
         \Comment{needs $\Gop v$}
  \State $\dot{h}^{(k)}_\scs \gets \textbf{(a)} + \textbf{(b)}$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent
$\Jlag$ runs neither algorithm. $\Jhoex$ runs the second in full. A partially implicit treatment
would keep \textbf{(a)} and drop the direction's increment with \textbf{(b)} --- lines 5 and 8,
and the $\dot{\delta}$ half of line 6 --- which costs no reconstruction of the direction at all;
it is not built here.

\subsection{Bound preservation, measured}

The schemes' claims are checked by instrumenting the limiter over a converged solve
($\sim$2M surface samples), counting how often the unlimited reconstruction leaves the two-node
interval, how often the limiter alters it, and how often the \emph{limited} value is still
outside:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
scheme & outside before & altered & outside after \\
\midrule
unlimited (control)        & 60.96\% & \phantom{0}0.00\% & 60.96\% \\
bounded-face clip          & 61.17\% & 61.17\% & 0.0000\% \\
Venkatakrishnan, $K=0$     & 61.11\% & 99.29\% & 0.0000\% \\
Darwish--Moukalled         & 61.13\% & 99.26\% & 0.0000\% \\
Venkatakrishnan, $K=5$     & 60.98\% & 65.84\% & 60.86\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The last row is the one to read carefully, and it is not a defect. With $\varepsilon^2 > 0$ the
limiter is deliberately inactive where the variation is at round-off, so it no longer enforces the
bound there --- "outside after" returns to the unlimited level. That is the trade $\varepsilon^2$
buys: bound preservation on the edges that matter, in exchange for not clipping the flow-aligned
ones where the two-node bound is meaningless. The two smooth limiters alter nearly every surface
($\sim$99\%) while the clip alters only those actually out of bounds, which is the difference
between a smooth rescaling and an active set.
