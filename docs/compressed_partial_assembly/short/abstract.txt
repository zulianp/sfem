Newton-Krylov solvers for finite-strain elasticity spend most of their time applying the tangent operator, and how that operator is stored decides both the time and the memory of a solve. We evaluate compressed formats along the whole range from the assembled matrix to matrix-free application: block sparse (BSR) matrices quantized per block, element matrices quantized per node pair, and partial assembly that stores the pulled-back material tangent at the quadrature points (on quadratic tetrahedra the ten weighted sums of it that the application uses), against BSR in double and single precision. The formats share fp16 and int8 encodings decoded inside the application, and a difference form that keeps rigid translations in the kernel of the encoded element and assembled matrices whatever the rounding. An encoding error enters inexact Newton as an additive forcing term; half precision and int8 mean-plus-deviation keep the exact iteration counts, int8 with one scale per element does not, and the quantized matrix fails without the difference form. On a 72-core Arm Neoverse-V2 CPU at 10^7 degrees of freedom, for linear and quadratic tetrahedra and trilinear and triquadratic hexahedra, the quantized sparse matrix is the fastest format on tetrahedra, solving in 0.73-0.92 of the time of single-precision BSR from 3.7-4.0x less operator memory; element matrices come close to it on trilinear and are ahead on triquadratic hexahedra, and they keep the lowest peak memory, since they need no assembled matrix. On one H100 GPU, against a BSR product 1.49-1.73x faster than cuSPARSE's fastest product, the quantized matrix leads on linear tetrahedra and trilinear hexahedra and the quadrature-point stores on the quadratic elements, since the matrices' assembly weighs more there.
