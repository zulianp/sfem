\title{A Filtered Nonlinear Multigrid Method for Stabilized Dual-Pass Mortar Contact}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We describe a nonlinear multigrid method for unilateral, frictionless contact
based on the stabilized dual-pass mortar discretization of Puso and Solberg.
The contact pressure is represented by localized nodal multiplier fields on
both potential contact surfaces.  For contact, these scalar nodal pressures
are converted into vector tractions by nodal normals, and stability is obtained
by penalizing the jump of the resulting traction vectors across the interface.
The displacement equation is solved by a shifted-penalty multigrid skeleton in
which nonlinear fine-level smoothing is combined with a recursively computed
coarse correction.  The central multigrid construction is a filtered coarse
active space: coarse corrections are excluded from the displacement supports
of currently active contact multipliers and their stabilization neighbors,
while the fine nonlinear relaxation remains free to update the active set.
For finite-strain elasticity, the same framework is combined with compressible
Neo--Hookean stresses, optional quasi-Newton material linearization during a
multigrid cycle, rediscretized coarse material tangents evaluated at projected
coarse displacements, and an inversion-aware globalization based on the
positivity of the deformation Jacobian.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Multigrid methods for contact mechanics must reconcile two conflicting
requirements.  Coarse levels are needed to eliminate long-wavelength elastic
error components, yet the unilateral contact constraint is geometrically local,
state-dependent, and often nonsmooth.  A naive coarse correction may introduce
normal jumps across an active contact interface, thereby exciting precisely
the high-stiffness modes that the contact stabilization is meant to control.
Conversely, over-constraining the fine problem would prevent the nonlinear
iteration from adapting the contact set.

The approach considered here follows a conservative principle.  The fine
problem is solved with the full stabilized active-set mortar residual and
tangent.  The coarse correction, however, is computed in a filtered active
space that is jump-free on the displacement supports of the current active
contact multipliers.  In its present form the filter removes both Cartesian
displacement components at active contact-adjacent nodes.  This is stronger
than the minimal normal-component filter, but it has the practical advantage
of being independent of a normal-tangential basis and therefore provides a
simple baseline for experiments.

The paper is organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:model} states the
finite-strain elastic model.  Section~\ref{sec:sp-contact} gives the
stabilized dual-pass mortar contact formulation.  Section~\ref{sec:filtered-space}
defines the filtered coarse active space and explains why it removes active
contact modes from the coarse correction.  Section~\ref{sec:mg} presents the
nonlinear multigrid algorithm.  Section~\ref{sec:coarse-tangents} discusses
coarse tangent construction, including rediscretization at projected coarse
displacements.  Section~\ref{sec:globalization} describes material
linearization, inversion-aware damping, and incremental loading.

\section{Elastic Model}
\label{sec:model}

Let $\Omega_0\subset\R^2$ be the reference configuration and let
$\ud:\Omega_0\to\R^2$ denote the displacement.  The deformation map is
\[
    \boldsymbol{\varphi}(X)=X+\ud(X),
    \qquad
    \FF(\ud)=\II+\nabla_X\ud,
    \qquad
    \JJ(\ud)=\det\FF(\ud).
\]
The finite-strain variant uses the compressible Neo--Hookean energy density
\[
    W(\FF)
    =
    \frac{\mu}{2}
    \left(
        \tr(\FF^T\FF)-2-2\log\JJ
    \right)
    +
    \frac{\lambda}{2}
    \left(\log\JJ\right)^2 ,
\]
where $\mu$ and $\lambda$ are the Lam'e parameters.  The first
Piola--Kirchhoff stress entering the reference-domain weak form is
\[
    \Pio(\FF)
    =
    \mu(\FF-\FF^{-T})
    +
    \lambda\log(\JJ)\FF^{-T}.
\]
For a test function $\vv$ in the homogeneous Dirichlet space, the elastic
residual is
\[
    \langle \rr_e(\ud),\vv\rangle
    =
    \int_{\Omega_0}
    \Pio(\FF(\ud)):\nabla_X\vv\,dX
    -
    \langle \ell_{\mathrm{ext}},\vv\rangle .
\]
The Cauchy stress used to evaluate physical normal stresses is
\[
    \SSig(\FF)
    =
    \frac{\mu}{\JJ}
    \left(\FF\FF^T-\II\right)
    +
    \frac{\lambda\log(\JJ)}{\JJ}\II .
\]
Admissible finite-strain states must satisfy $\JJ>0$ elementwise.  This
condition is not merely a diagnostic: it is required for the energy, stress,
and tangent to remain well defined.

\section{Stabilized Dual-Pass Mortar Contact}
\label{sec:sp-contact}

Let $\Gamma_c^1$ and $\Gamma_c^2$ denote the two potential contact traces.
Following Puso and Solberg, the multiplier space is not chosen from one
privileged mortar side.  Instead,
\[
    M_h=M_h^1\times M_h^2,
    \qquad
    \lambda_h=(\lambda_h^1,\lambda_h^2),
\]
where each $M_h^i$ is the localized trace space on $\Gamma_c^i$.  For the
linear triangular prototype this gives a continuous piecewise-linear scalar
pressure on each contact edge trace.  If $N_{b,h}^i$ is the set of contact
boundary nodes on side $i$, then
\[
    \lambda_h^i(x)
    =
    \sum_{A\in N_{b,h}^i}
    \varphi_A^i(x)\lambda_A^i\nn_A^i ,
    \qquad x\in\Gamma_c^i .
\]
The unknown $\lambda_A^i\in\R$ is non-negative for unilateral frictionless
contact, and $\nn_A^i$ is a nodal normal obtained by averaging adjacent
oriented edge normals and normalizing.  Thus the unknown field is scalar, but
the physical traction entering the weak form is vector-valued.

For every overlapping pair of contact edges the method performs two directed
integrations.  In the first pass, side $1$ supplies the multiplier test
function and side $2$ supplies the opposing displacement trace; in the second
pass the roles are reversed.  The resulting discrete coupling can be written
as
\[
    b(\lambda_h,\vv_h)=\lambda^T B^T v,
\]
where $B^T u-g_0$ is the vector of physical normal gap moments associated
with the localized multiplier basis.  In the present sign convention, positive
gap means separation and negative gap means penetration.

The dual-pass multiplier space contains two pressure descriptions of the same
contact traction.  Stability is obtained by the Solberg-Puso traction-jump
term
\[
    j(\mu_h,\lambda_h)
    =
    \frac{1}{2}
    \sum_p
    \gamma_p h_p
    \left[
    \int_{\Gamma^{p,12}}
    \mu_h^1\cdot(\lambda_h^1+\lambda_h^2)\,d\Gamma
    +
    \int_{\Gamma^{p,21}}
    \mu_h^2\cdot(\lambda_h^2+\lambda_h^1)\,d\Gamma
    \right],
\]
which has the matrix form $\mu^T J\lambda$.  Because the normal directions on
the two sides are opposite in the continuum limit, equal scalar pressures on
the two sides produce equal and opposite vector tractions and therefore a
small stabilized jump.  The stabilization parameter is written
$\gamma_p=\alpha/E_p$ with a dimensionless $\alpha$ of order one and a
representative pair modulus $E_p$.

With geometry frozen during one nonlinear contact evaluation, the stabilized
unilateral conditions are
\[
    R_u(u,\lambda)=R_e(u)-B\lambda=0,
\]
\[
    g(u,\lambda)=B^T u+J\lambda-g_0\ge 0,
    \qquad
    \lambda\ge 0,
    \qquad
    \lambda\cdot g(u,\lambda)=0 .
\]
The augmented-Lagrangian displacement residual used by the nonlinear
multigrid smoother replaces $\lambda$ by the projected active pressure
\[
    p_\rho(u,\lambda)
    =
    \max\{0,\lambda-\rho\,g(u,\lambda)\},
\]
with row-wise penalty $\rho>0$.  Active multiplier nodes are those for which
$p_\rho>0$.  The associated displacement tangent contains the positive
semidefinite active contribution
\[
    H_c
    =
    \sum_{A\in\calA}
    m_A\rho_A\,B_A B_A^T ,
\]
where $m_A$ is the lumped contact mass of the multiplier basis function and
$B_A$ is the frozen gap derivative.  The multiplier update is a damped
projected augmentation,
\[
    \lambda^{k+1}
    =
    \max\{0,\lambda^k-\omega_\lambda\rho\,g(u^{k+1},\lambda^k)\},
\]
and therefore the active release condition includes the stabilization term
$J\lambda$, as required by the stabilized KKT system.

\section{Filtered Coarse Active Space}
\label{sec:filtered-space}

Let level $\ell=0$ denote the finest discretization and let increasing
$\ell$ denote coarser levels.  The nodal displacement space on level $\ell$ is
$V_\ell\subset [H^1(\Omega_0)]^2$, with nodal vector
$\ud_\ell\in\R^{2n_\ell}$.  The geometric prolongation from level $\ell+1$ to
level $\ell$ is denoted by
\[
    \II_{\ell+1}^{\ell}:\R^{2n_{\ell+1}}\to\R^{2n_\ell},
    \qquad
    \RR_{\ell}^{\ell+1}=(\II_{\ell+1}^{\ell})^T .
\]

For a frozen active set on level $\ell$, define the side-one active node set
and the side-two active support set by
\[
    \calA_{1,\ell}
    =
    \{a(c):c\in\calC_\ell,\ \alpha_c=1\},
\]
\[
    \calA_{2,\ell}
    =
    \{j:j\in\supp \PP_\ell^c,\ c\in\calC_\ell,\ \alpha_c=1\},
\]
where $\PP_\ell^c$ is the interpolation row for the opposite trace.  In an
unbiased contact description the side labels are local to a row, and both
sides must therefore be treated symmetrically.  The active nodal set is
\[
    \calA_\ell = \calA_{1,\ell}\cup\calA_{2,\ell}.
\]
The present filtered space removes all displacement components at these
nodes:
\[
    d_\ell^a =
    \begin{cases}
    0, & a\in\calA_\ell,\\
    1, & a\notin\calA_\ell,
    \end{cases}
    \qquad
    \ZZ_\ell=\diag(\dd_\ell)\otimes \II_2 .
\]
Dirichlet degrees of freedom are included in the constrained set as usual.
The admissible coarse correction space is
\[
    \widetilde V_\ell
    =
    \{\ZZ_\ell\vv_\ell:\vv_\ell\in V_\ell\}.
\]

This construction is deliberately conservative.  A minimal contact filter
would rotate each active nodal correction into a normal-tangential coordinate
system, eliminate only the active normal component, and rotate the resulting
correction back to physical coordinates.  Such a construction preserves
tangential motion and reflects the fact that the coarse level does not lose
``$x$ and $y$'' as physical directions; it loses the active normal component.
The Cartesian filter studied here is stronger: it eliminates both physical
components at active contact-adjacent nodes.  It is nevertheless a useful
baseline because it is simple, symmetric with respect to the two contact
sides, and independent of normal averaging ambiguities at nodes shared by
several contact rows.

\subsection{Vanishing of Active Contact Modes}

The purpose of the filter is to ensure that the coarse correction carries no
active normal jump.  For any active row $c$ and any vector $\vv_\ell$, the
filtered vector satisfies
\[
    (\ZZ_\ell\vv_\ell)_1^c=\boldsymbol{0},
    \qquad
    (\ZZ_\ell\vv_\ell)_2^j=\boldsymbol{0}
    \quad \forall j\in\supp\PP_\ell^c .
\]
Consequently,
\[
    {\nn^c}^{T}
    \left(
        (\ZZ_\ell\vv_\ell)_2^c
        -
        (\ZZ_\ell\vv_\ell)_1^c
    \right)
    =
    0,
\]
where the second trace is understood through the interpolation row
$\PP_\ell^c$.  Since $\delta P_n^c$ vanishes for corrections in
$\widetilde V_\ell$, the active contact Hessian has no action on this space:
\[
    \ZZ_\ell^T\HH_{c,\ell}\ZZ_\ell=\boldsymbol{0}
\]
for the frozen active-set, frozen-geometry correction problem.  The coarse
operator can therefore focus on the elastic error components that remain
compatible with the current contact constraints.

\subsection{Transfer of Active Information}

The active mask is detected on the finest level from the current Nitsche
state.  Coarser masks are obtained by the support of the transpose
prolongation,
\[
    s_{\ell+1}
    =
    \chi\!\left((\II_{\ell+1}^{\ell})^T s_\ell\right),
    \qquad
    d_{\ell+1}=1-s_{\ell+1},
\]
where $\chi(z)=1$ for positive entries and $\chi(z)=0$ otherwise.  Thus a
coarse node is filtered if its basis function overlaps any filtered fine
node.  This choice is intentionally one-sided: the multigrid hierarchy does
not recompute a different coarse active set from a coarse contact problem.
Only the active supports and their connected neighbors are filtered, while the
elastic operator is transferred or rediscretized according to the tangent
strategy described below.

\section{Nonlinear Multigrid Algorithm}
\label{sec:mg}

The finest-level nonlinear equation is
\[
    \rr_0(\ud_0)=\rr_{e,0}(\ud_0)+\rr_{c,0}(\ud_0)=\boldsymbol{0}.
\]
An outer nonlinear iteration applies a bounded number of nonlinear multigrid
cycles until the residual norm reaches the desired tolerance.  A cycle begins
by refreshing the fine residual, tangent, active set, and filtered hierarchy.
It then performs nonlinear pre-smoothing, computes a restricted residual,
solves a recursive masked coarse correction problem, applies the prolongated
coarse correction, and finishes with nonlinear post-smoothing.

Let $\nu_1$ and $\nu_2$ be the number of pre- and post-smoothing steps.  On
the fine level, one nonlinear smoothing step computes
\[
    \rr_0^k=\rr_0(\ud_0^k),
    \qquad
    \HH_0^k=\nabla\rr_0(\ud_0^k),
\]
and applies a fixed number of stationary linear relaxation sweeps to
\[
    \HH_0^k\boldsymbol{s}^k\approx -\rr_0^k,
    \qquad
    \ud_0^{k+1}=\ud_0^k+\boldsymbol{s}^k .
\]
The fine smoother is constrained only by Dirichlet conditions.  Active contact
nodes remain free on the fine level, so that the nonlinear iteration can
change the active set.

The recursive correction on a generic level $\ell$ is linear with respect to
the tangent available for the current cycle.  Given a masked right-hand side
$\bb_\ell$ and tangent $\HH_\ell$, the correction satisfies
\[
    \du_\ell|_{\calM_\ell}=0,
    \qquad
    \HH_\ell\du_\ell \approx \bb_\ell
    \quad\text{on unconstrained degrees of freedom}.
\]
The multigrid recursion is
\[
    \du_\ell^{(0)}=\boldsymbol{0},
\]
\[
    \du_\ell^{(1)}
    =
    S_\ell^{\nu_1}(\HH_\ell,\bb_\ell,\du_\ell^{(0)}),
\]
\[
    \boldsymbol{\rho}_\ell
    =
    \bb_\ell-\HH_\ell\du_\ell^{(1)},
    \qquad
    \boldsymbol{\rho}_\ell|_{\calM_\ell}=0,
\]
\[
    \bb_{\ell+1}
    =
    (\II_{\ell+1}^{\ell})^T\boldsymbol{\rho}_\ell,
    \qquad
    \du_{\ell+1}
    =
    \operatorname{cycle}_{\ell+1}(\bb_{\ell+1}),
\]
\[
    \du_\ell^{(2)}
    =
    \du_\ell^{(1)}
    +
    \II_{\ell+1}^{\ell}\du_{\ell+1},
    \qquad
    \du_\ell^{(2)}|_{\calM_\ell}=0,
\]
\[
    \du_\ell
    =
    S_\ell^{\nu_2}(\HH_\ell,\bb_\ell,\du_\ell^{(2)}).
\]
On the coarsest level the masked system is solved directly on the
unconstrained degrees of freedom.  Repeating the coarse-correction stage gives
a W-cycle; a single recursive visit gives a V-cycle.

\section{Coarse Tangent Construction}
\label{sec:coarse-tangents}

Two coarse tangent constructions are relevant.

\paragraph{Inherited tangent.}
For a fixed fine-level tangent, the Galerkin hierarchy is
\[
    \HH_{\ell+1}
    =
    (\II_{\ell+1}^{\ell})^T
    \HH_\ell
    \II_{\ell+1}^{\ell}.
\]
This is the variationally consistent coarse representation of the current
linearized problem.  It requires no additional material or contact
reassembly.  Once the active space has been filtered, the Galerkin operator is
used only on the compatible correction space.

\paragraph{Rediscretized tangent.}
For nonlinear finite-strain problems, the coarse tangent may instead be
assembled directly on level $\ell+1$ at a projected coarse displacement:
\[
    \HH_{\ell+1}
    =
    \nabla\rr_{\ell+1}(\ud_{\ell+1}),
    \qquad
    \ud_{\ell+1}=Q_\ell\ud_\ell .
\]
The inexpensive projection used in the present experiments is nodal
injection.  For nested meshes,
\[
    \ud_{\ell+1}(X_{\ell+1}^a)
    =
    \ud_\ell(X_{\ell+1}^a).
\]
No mesh-volume scaling is applied.  Displacement is a pointwise kinematic
quantity, so the injected coarse value has the same physical units as the
fine value.  Scaling would be appropriate for transferred residuals or
integrated loads, not for nodal displacement values.  Strong Dirichlet values
are imposed after the projection.

Rediscretization changes the mathematical coarse problem: it no longer
represents the exact Galerkin projection of the fine tangent.  Its purpose is
to provide a cheaper nonlinear coarse model of the current deformed state,
especially when material stiffness changes substantially with deformation.
The filtered active space is retained in either case; only the construction
of the elastic and contact tangent differs.

\section{Material Linearization and Globalization}
\label{sec:globalization}

For hyperelasticity, the residual is always evaluated at the current
displacement.  The material tangent may be refreshed at every residual/tangent
evaluation or frozen for the duration of a nonlinear multigrid cycle.  The
latter gives a quasi-Newton cycle:
\[
    \HH_{e,0}^{\mathrm{cycle}}
    =
    \nabla\rr_{e,0}(\ud_0^{\mathrm{start}}),
    \qquad
    \rr_{e,0}=\rr_{e,0}(\ud_0)
    \text{ at each current iterate}.
\]
This avoids repeatedly rebuilding the finite-strain material tangent during
the internal smoothing steps, while still measuring the nonlinear residual at
the updated state.

Large coarse corrections can invalidate the finite-strain model by producing
elements with $\JJ\le0$.  The coarse correction is therefore optionally
damped by an inversion-aware line search.  Given a proposed correction
$\du_0$, the accepted step is
\[
    \ud_0^+ = \ud_0+\alpha\du_0,
    \qquad
    \alpha\in\{1,\beta,\beta^2,\ldots\},
\]
where the largest tested $\alpha$ satisfying
\[
    \min_{K\in\calT_0}\det\FF_K(\ud_0+\alpha\du_0)
    >
    J_{\min}
\]
is chosen.  This globalization enforces admissibility of the deformation but
does not require monotone residual decrease.  It is therefore less restrictive
than a residual Armijo search and is targeted specifically at preventing
inverted hyperelastic elements.

The same admissibility test is useful outside the nonlinear solver through
incremental loading.  Let $\bar \ud_D$ be the target imposed displacement.
The loading process solves a sequence of problems with
\[
    \ud_D^{(m)} = t_m\bar\ud_D,
    \qquad
    0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_M=1 .
\]
Each converged solution initializes the next load level.  If a load step
causes loss of admissibility, the increment is reduced and retried.  This
continuation strategy separates global deformation robustness from the local
multigrid correction mechanism.

\section{Expected Diagnostics}

The primary convergence measure is the norm of the finest-level nonlinear
residual after applying Dirichlet constraints.  For the contact problem, this
residual should be interpreted together with the active pressure $p_c$, the
gap field, and the minimum element Jacobian.  A nonzero elastic normal stress
outside the active region is not necessarily a contact-force error; the
inactive contact contribution is zero by construction.  The relevant contact
quantity outside the active set is the active pressure, which should vanish
there.

Several mechanisms can obstruct residual reduction.  Insufficient nonlinear
smoothing leaves high-frequency active-set changes unresolved before the
coarse correction is applied.  A coarse space that is too restrictive may
remove legitimate low-frequency elastic modes near the contact zone.  A
rediscretized coarse tangent may depart from the fine Galerkin correction
enough to reduce coarse-grid efficiency.  A frozen material tangent can
behave like a weak quasi-Newton approximation under large deformation.
Finally, damping based only on element invertibility may accept steps that are
admissible but not residual reducing; this is a robustness device, not a
convergence guarantee.

\section{Conclusions}

The method combines three ideas: a Nitsche active-set formulation of
frictionless contact, a jump-free filtered coarse active space, and a
nonlinear multigrid cycle with optional finite-strain globalization.  The
filtered space gives a clear algebraic mechanism for preventing active
contact jumps from entering the coarse correction.  Galerkin coarsening
preserves the current fine linearization, whereas rediscretized coarse
tangents provide a deformation-dependent alternative for hyperelasticity.
For larger deformations, inversion-aware damping and incremental loading are
natural safeguards because they enforce the fundamental admissibility
condition $\det\FF>0$.  A normal-tangential filtered space remains the next
mathematically sharper refinement: it would remove only the active normal
component while preserving tangential coarse motion.
